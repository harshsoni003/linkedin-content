# Hook Families

Seven ways to open. Spread your attempts across at least five — ten versions of one safe opening is one opening in ten outfits.

| Family | Looks like | Wins when |
|---|---|---|
| **First person** | "I stopped billing hourly in January." | You did something real with your name on it |
| **Bare number** | "34 clients." | One number is startling enough to stand alone |
| **This X / Your X** | "Your books are lying to you." | The reader owns the cost or the capability |
| **Corrective** | "Clean books and compliant books are not the same thing." | You can prove a common belief wrong |
| **Unfinished story** | "The email came in at 11pm." | A real moment carries the change, ending can wait |
| **Product title** | "The 2026 pricing guide is here." | The thing itself is the news — best for lead posts |
| **Tool as subject** | "Claude can now..." | The news is what a tool just became able to do |

## Worked example

Same story, seven angles. A bookkeeper found $48,000 in unfiled sales tax in a new client's first week.

> **First person** — "I had to tell a new client they owed $48,000 the state didn't know about yet."
>
> **Bare number** — "$48,000 in unfiled sales tax.
> The client thought their books were clean."
>
> **Bare number, stacked** — "3 states. 14 months. $48,000. All missed by the last bookkeeper."
>
> **Unfinished story** — "Day 6 of onboarding. My senior accountant called me over. She'd found something in the 2024 filings."
>
> **Corrective** — "Clean books and compliant books are not the same thing. The gap cost one brand $48,000."
>
> **Your X** — "Your books can look perfect and still be hiding a $48,000 problem."

Notice: every one uses the **same real facts**. Nothing invented.

## Never open with

- "Most people think..."
- "Here's what..."
- "I have a confession"
- "Let me tell you"
- "Unpopular opinion"
- "Imagine..."

These could start anyone's post. State the surprising thing directly instead of announcing that a correction is coming.

## Ranking your options

Three questions:

1. **Clarity** — does a stranger know what this is about in two seconds?
2. **Curiosity** — could they guess the ending without tapping? If yes, it drops.
3. **Honesty** — can the body pay off exactly what this line promises?

The winner is the intersection of all three, not the cleverest line.
