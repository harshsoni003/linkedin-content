## The post

I shipped [YOUR NUMBER: how many projects last year] projects last year.

Not one of them was stopped by the code.

The hard part started after the build.

I can take an idea to a live product in days. Auth, database, payments, agents,
deploy. That part I know cold.

Then it ships, and nobody comes.

Marketing is a separate job I never learned, and SEO is a slower one sitting on
top of it. Both of them ask me to show up on a day when nothing is happening and
do it again anyway, for months, before anything moves.

I'm bad at that right now.

So I read the builders who aren't. [CONFIRM NAMES: Marc Lou, Pieter Levels,
Tibo — check spelling before posting]. They build what I build. They just never
stop putting it in front of people.

That's the gap I'm closing this year, same energy I put into shipping, pointed
at distribution instead.

If you've taken a product from no traffic to real traffic, what actually moved
it?

## Alternate openings

**A — bare number**
> [N] projects shipped last year.
> [N2] of them ever got found by a stranger.

*Shape: bare number. Use only if you have the second number and the gap between
them is stark. Strongest version of this post if those two figures exist.*

**B — corrective**
> Shipping fast is not the advantage everyone tells you it is.
> I can build in days what takes a team a month, and it changed nothing about
> whether anyone found the thing.

*Shape: corrective. Widest reach, most arguing in the comments. Least about you,
so it wins attention and builds less trust.*

**C — unfinished story**
> I deployed on a Thursday night and opened analytics on Sunday.
> [YOUR NUMBER: visits] visits, most of them mine.

*Shape: unfinished story. The most human of the four and the most likely to get
replies, but it needs a real moment and a real number. Do not write it from
memory of how it felt.*

## The call

Run the main one. It opens on your own work with your name on it, which is what
your profile does naturally, and the question at the end is answerable in one
line, which is what actually gets you tips instead of "great post."

Use C instead if you can remember one specific launch and its real first-week
traffic. A concrete dead launch beats a general admission every time.

## Voice notes

Applied from the profile:
- Opens on the build, no run-up
- Stack listed, not described ("auth, database, payments, agents, deploy")
- Problem stated flat, no cushion ("I'm bad at that right now") — same move as
  "i need 1 more day to fix everything, sorry for this problem"
- No em dashes, no exclamation points, no "journey"
- Long explaining sentence, then a short landing line

One deliberate break from the profile: your profile says you end on the build,
not on a lesson. This post ends on a question. That is the goal overriding the
habit — you asked for tips, and a post that ends on the build gets applause
instead of answers. The line before the question still lands on the build, so
the habit is kept where it can be.

## Still missing

Numbers, again. This post has one blank where a number goes and it is carrying
the whole opening. Your profile flags this as your single biggest gap and this
draft is where it costs the most: "I shipped a lot of projects" is a feeling,
"I shipped 11" is evidence. Same for the traffic figure in opening C.
