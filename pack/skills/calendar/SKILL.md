# Calendar

Which posts you have is one problem. The order and spacing you release them in is a separate problem. The common failure is accidental: publish several posts in a few days, go quiet, then restart with no reason for either choice. This skill spreads a finished batch across the period you choose and gives a practical reason for every date.

**When to use:** the moment a batch of drafts is finished and checked, or when you look at your profile and see three posts in one week followed by dead air.
**Time it takes:** about 5 minutes.
**What you end with:** a dated table, one row per post, a reason on every row, and a plain list of anything that would not fit.

## What to give it

**1. Your drafts, or just their titles, each marked gated or ungated.** A gated post asks readers to comment a word to get something, a guide, a template, a checklist. An ungated post gives its value in the post itself, with no ask. Good input looks like this:

> Use calendar on these 11 drafts, starting September 1.
> 1. "I moved all 34 clients off hourly billing in January." UNGATED
> 2. "The E-commerce Sales Tax Checklist" GATED, comment word CLEAN
> 3. "Your Shopify payout report is hiding four numbers." UNGATED
> [8 more]

**2. Your start date, preferred posting frequency, and any days you cannot or will not post.**

Example: "Start September 1. Three posts a week. Nothing on weekends."

**3. Optional: your scoreboard output.** If scoreboard found a rhythm pattern in your history, paste it and the plan will use it. Without it, the plan runs on the craft rules below, which is a fine place to start.

## The method

The plan holds three working rules. They are planning defaults, not performance findings. The user's stated preference and their own results outrank them.

**Rule 1: use the user's preferred frequency.** If none is supplied, spread the ready posts evenly across the requested period and label that spacing as a planning default. Do not invent weak posts to fill empty dates.

**Rule 2: do not place two gated posts consecutively.** Put at least one ungated post between posts that ask readers to comment for something. Use wider spacing only when the user's own results or stated campaign plan supports it.

**Rule 3: vary subjects and post types.** Do not cluster three posts about the same subject or three posts with the same opening shape when the batch gives you alternatives.

### Step 1. Count what you have

Total drafts, how many gated, how many ungated. If gated outnumber ungated more than two to one, say so before dating anything. The schedule cannot fix a batch that is all asks; that gets fixed by writing, not by spacing.

### Step 2. Place the gated posts first

Place them first so the plan can keep at least one ungated post between them. If there are more gated posts than the period can hold without placing two consecutively, name which ones roll into the next period rather than crowding them in.

### Step 3. Fill with ungated posts

Spread the ungated posts across the remaining dates at the user's preferred frequency. If no frequency was supplied, space them evenly. Alternate subjects and opening shapes where the batch allows it. Do not pad the calendar with invented posts.

### Step 4. Check the batch against the window

If the batch leaves large gaps, show the gaps instead of inventing posts. Offer two choices: keep the lighter schedule or prepare more drafts. Do not describe either option as better without the user's own results.

### Step 5. Times of day, only with evidence

Recommend a time only when the user's scoreboard supports it. Otherwise leave the time open or use a time the user already follows, clearly labeled as their operating choice rather than a performance claim.

### Step 6. Return the dated table

| Date | Day | Post | Gated | Why this slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Every row carries its reason. A calendar without a reason per row is a guess with dates on it, and a guess gets rearranged the first time you feel like posting something else.

## Output

1. Batch summary: total drafts, gated, ungated, and any imbalance called out.
2. The dated table, one reason per row.
3. Anything rolled to next month, and why.
4. Any planning rule that could not be held, named explicitly.

## What good looks like

Pick any row and ask "why this date?" The table should answer without you in the room. Then check the gated rows: if two appear consecutively, the plan failed its clearest rule.

## Guardrails

- Never place two gated posts consecutively.
- Never pad the schedule to hit a post count. An empty slot beats a weak post.
- Never invent a draft, a title, or a comment word to fill a gap.
- Never claim a day or time will perform better without evidence from the user's own results.
- If a rule had to break, say which one and why. Never break one silently.

## Works even better with

- **voice-firewall** checks each draft before it earns a date. Only checked drafts deserve slots.
- **tracker** records each post's slot and refuses to mark anything Dated without one.
- **scoreboard** reads the results at the end of the month, so next month's calendar runs on your numbers instead of the defaults.
