# Voice firewall

Readers have learned to spot machine writing, because they scroll past it all day. When your post carries the tells, they do not think "AI helped with this." They think "this person has nothing to say," and that judgment sticks to your name, not to the tool. The tells are specific, countable, and fixable, and most checkers stop at pointing them out. This one is an editor, not a critic: every line it flags comes back already rewritten in your voice, and you leave with the clean version, not a to-do list.

**When to use:** on every draft before it goes public, no exceptions. Also on an older post that underperformed when the reason is not obvious.
**Time it takes:** about 3 minutes.
**What you end with:** a verdict, every weak line rewritten in your voice, and a clean draft. It is ready to publish only after the verdict is PASS and you confirm every factual claim.

## What to give it

**1. The finished draft, full text.** Not a summary of it, the actual post. Good input looks like this:

> Run voice-firewall on this draft:
> Most founders think bookkeeping is an expense. It's not an expense, it's an investment.
> Last month a client asked us to cut our fee. Instead we went through his books.
> [the rest of the draft]

**2. Optional: your voice profile.** If you have your voice profile from voice-match, paste it. If not, paste one post that sounds like you. The check works without either; the rewrites just have to lean on the best lines already inside the draft instead of on you.

## The method

### Step 1. Read it as a stranger and score the voice

Give it a voice score out of 10. A 10 sounds unmistakably like the writer. A 1 sounds generic and machine-written. Be strict and explain the score in one sentence.

### Step 2. Quote every line that gives it away

For each one: the line verbatim, the tell it trips, and a rewrite in the author's voice. The rewrite comes from the voice profile if one was pasted, from the pasted sample if not, and from the strongest human lines already in the draft as the last resort.

The tells that matter:

| Tell | Why it reads as machine |
|---|---|
| "It's not X, it's Y", including the stacked version "It's not X. It's not Y. It's Z." | The single most recognizable AI construction |
| Parallel repetition: consecutive lines opening with the same word or built on the same skeleton. "More leads. More calls. More revenue." | A looping rhythm people rarely produce. Humans escalate; models echo |
| Triads everywhere | A three-item list in every paragraph is a model habit, not a human one |
| Rhetorical questions as transitions: "So what does this actually mean?" | Filler dressed up as engagement. A person just says the thing |
| Vendor grammar: "seamless", "leverage", "unlock", "game-changer", "elevate", "delve", "powered by" | Nobody talks like this about their own work |
| Filler transitions: "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "At the end of the day", "In today's fast-paced world", "More on that later" | Phrases that add nothing and appear in generic generated writing |
| The pick-two-audiences frame: "Whether you're a founder or a freelancer..." | Written for everyone, so it lands with no one |
| "Imagine..." openers | Generic by design. A real moment beats an imagined one every time |
| Manufactured hedges: "roughly", "approximately", "about" on a number the author knows exactly | Drains the claim. If it was $4,310, say $4,310 |
| Celebrating an absence: "0 drafts lost" | Hands the reader a non-event to feel nothing about |
| Uniform sentence length | Humans vary. Models do not |
| A conclusion that restates the opening | Adds nothing and reads like a book report |

Two rules for the rewrites:

- **The fix for repetition stacks is escalation.** Each line must add new information, raise the stakes, or move the argument forward. Keep the strongest line, rewrite the echoes.
- **Honest hedges stay.** If the author genuinely talks in qualifiers and the voice profile or sample shows it, that is their voice. This step removes AI-invented hedging, not the way a person actually speaks.

### Step 3. Check where the draft goes abstract

Winning posts tend to carry real numbers on far more of their lines than losing posts do. Walk the draft and mark every line holding an abstraction where the author's material had a figure: "significant savings" when they know it was $9,800, "a few clients" when it was four. Surface each one with the concrete version.

Then stop. Forcing a digit onto every line reads as a spreadsheet and fails this skill's own test. Only numbers from the author's material ever go in. Never invent one to strengthen a line.

### Step 4. Mechanical checks

- **Punctuation must match the writer.** If the voice profile says the writer avoids em dashes, replace them. If the supplied samples use them consistently, preserve that habit. If there is no evidence, prefer a period or comma.
- Every claim traceable to something real the author said.
- No number that did not come from the author's material.
- No emoji unless the author's own writing uses them.
- No dense five-sentence paragraph blocks. Line breaks are a pacing tool.
- Exclamation points near zero. Urgency comes from the logic and the proof, not the punctuation.

### Step 5. The contradiction check

Read line 1 against line 2, then line 2 against the body, then the ending against the opening. Name any place where a later line undoes an earlier claim. This is the failure that kills otherwise strong posts, and the author is the least likely person to spot it in their own writing.

### Step 6. Verdict, then the clean version

PASS or FAIL. Fail it if the score is under 7, punctuation clearly fights the supplied voice, there is a contradiction, or a claim cannot be defended.

Either way, deliver the full draft with every rewrite applied. If the verdict is FAIL or a claim still needs confirmation, label the draft "not ready to publish" and name what remains.

## Output

1. Voice score out of 10, where 10 sounds unmistakably like the writer.
2. Every flagged line, quoted, with the tell named and the rewrite in the author's voice.
3. Lines that went abstract where the material had a real figure, with the concrete version.
4. Punctuation check: what did not match the supplied voice, and what changed.
5. Contradictions found, or "none".
6. PASS or FAIL, with the reason.
7. The full clean draft with every fix applied, labeled either "ready for your final read" or "not ready to publish," with the reason.

## What good looks like

Read the clean version next to the original. Every change should sound more like you, not just less like AI. If a rewrite sounds like a second robot with a different accent, the skill did not have enough of your voice to work with: paste your voice profile or one real post and rerun it.

## Guardrails

- Never impose a punctuation preference that contradicts the writer's real samples. When no preference is visible, use periods and commas.
- Never rewrite the author's meaning while fixing their phrasing. A claim that seems wrong gets flagged as a question, never silently changed.
- Never invent a number, name, or fact to make a line stronger.
- Never push numbers onto every line.
- Never flag the author's own natural phrasing as a tell just because AI also uses it. Their real writing outranks the table.
- Never claim a checked post is guaranteed to read as human to every reader. This check removes the known tells; it does not make writing bulletproof.
