# Hook writer

The first line decides whether a reader reaches the rest of the post. Most people write it last, as an afterthought, or write ten versions that are really one safe opening in ten outfits. This skill brings the opening patterns and the unanswered-question rule, so you bring one idea and leave with 10 genuinely different openings, ranked, with the winner named.

**When to use:** you have the idea and need the first line, or a post went out and nobody stopped for it.
**Time it takes:** about 3 minutes.
**What you end with:** 10 openings ranked best to last, each labeled with its family and why it fits your idea, plus the one to ship and what the body of the post owes it.

## What to give it

**1. Your idea, 1 to 3 sentences about something real.**

Example of good input:

> "A new client came in thinking their books were clean. In the first week we found $48,000 in unfiled sales tax across 3 states. Their old bookkeeper had never registered them anywhere."

**2. Any real numbers and details around it. The more the better.**

Example: "34 clients total, the cleanup took 6 weeks, the exposure covered 14 months of sales."

**3. Optional: your voice profile.** If you have your voice profile from voice-match, paste it. If not, paste one post that sounds like you. Without either, the openings will be sharp but they will sound like a strong writer, not specifically like you.

## The method

### Step 1. Strip the idea down to its concrete parts

List every number, name, moment, cost, and timeframe in what the user pasted. These are the only raw materials. If there is not a single concrete element, stop and ask for one real detail before writing anything. A hook built on abstraction is a hook built on air.

### Step 2. Find the gap

A hook works by opening a gap the reader's brain cannot leave open: a question, a contradiction, a tension. Before writing, answer this: after reading one line, what would a stranger need to know? That need is the hook. Two hard rules:

- **The question must not resolve before "see more."** If the reader can guess the answer from the visible lines, the opening has given away the post. Do not state the explanation, fix, or payoff inside that block.
- **Keep the visible opening concise.** The preview varies by device and layout. Do not spend it greeting the audience or introducing the topic.

### Step 3. Write 10 openings across the families

Use this family table. Spread the 10 across at least five families, with no more than two per family.

| Family | What it looks like | When it wins |
|---|---|---|
| First person | Opens with a thing you did. "I built", "I spent", "We fired" | You did something with a real result and your name on it |
| Bare product title | Names the thing you made, no verb about you. "The 2026 pricing guide is here" | The thing itself is the news; strongest for lead posts |
| Bare number first | Opens on a number with no verb. "34 clients." | One number is startling enough to stand alone |
| This X / Your X | Speaks straight at something the reader owns. "Your books are lying to you" | The reader owns the cost or the capability |
| Corrective | States a surprising truth against a common belief | You can prove the common belief wrong |
| Tool as subject | The tool does the acting. "Claude can now..." | The news is what a tool just became able to do |
| Unfinished story | Opens inside a moment with the ending withheld. "The email came in at 11pm." | A real moment carries the change, and the ending can wait |

Each opening is the full visible block: the hook line, plus a second line when it earns its place. The second line must add information, by raising the stakes, reversing the expected explanation, adding scope, or making the cost personal. If deleting it changes nothing, it is repetition; cut it.

On numbers: use one when it makes the event or result more concrete. Choose the number most important to the idea. Use only numbers the user gave. Never invent one.

### Step 4. Label every opening

Family, plus one line on why it fits this specific idea. "This shape usually performs" is not a why.

### Step 5. Rank all 10 and pick

Rank on three questions:

- **Clarity.** Does a stranger know what this is about within two seconds?
- **Curiosity.** Could they guess the ending without tapping? If yes, it drops.
- **Honesty.** Can the body pay off exactly what this line promises, with the material given?

The winner is the intersection of all three, not the cleverest line. State the pick, then in one line, what the body must now deliver to keep the promise.

## Output

A numbered list, ranked 1 to 10. Each entry:

- The opening exactly as it would appear, line breaks included
- Family
- Why it fits, in one line

Then two closing lines: the pick, and what the body owes it.

## What good looks like

Take the number one opening and hide the rest of the idea. If a stranger reading it cold could guess the ending, the gap resolved early; demote it. If they cannot even tell what the post is about, it is too clever; demote it. The winner survives both readings.

## Guardrails

- Every fact, number, and name comes from what the user pasted. Never invent one.
- Never open with "Most people think", "Here's what", "I have a confession", "Let me tell you", or "Unpopular opinion". The corrective family states the surprising truth directly instead of announcing that a correction is coming.
- No generic intensifiers. "Insane", "massive", "game-changing" create no stakes; the specific fact does.
- Never resolve the gap inside the visible block.
- Ten openings means ten different attacks on the idea, not one attack rephrased. No two openings share a first word.
- If the idea has no concrete element, ask for one real detail instead of writing vague hooks.
