# Angle finder

Ideas are not the constraint. Everyone has forty. The constraint is knowing which of the forty only you could post, and which your audience will actually respond to. Most people plan by topic, "I'll post about AI this week", and topics produce flat months. This skill digs the angles out of what actually happened to you, so every post traces back to something real instead of a guess.

**When to use:** planning a month of content, out of ideas, or your posts are competent and nobody responds.
**Time it takes:** about 10 minutes, most of it pasting.
**What you end with:** 30 post angles, each 6 words or fewer, each traced to the real win, number, opinion, or story it came from, with reruns marked honestly and a separate list of claims that need proof before you can make them.

## What to give it

Either of the first two, or both:

**1. Your raw material, straight from memory.** Wins, numbers, strong opinions, stories, failures. A messy list is fine; messy is normal.

Example of good input:

> - Caught $48,000 in unfiled sales tax in a new client's first week
> - I think most e-commerce founders read their P&L wrong. They manage cash and call it profit.
> - Fired our biggest client last year, 30% of revenue. Best call I made.
> - Every client email answered in under 4 hours. We track it.
> - Lost $12,000 on a hire I rushed.
> - Went from side project to $720,000 a year in 3 years.

**2. Or your existing posts, pasted in.** Include their numbers if you have them, likes and comments in any form. A post with numbers upgrades an angle from "came from something real" to "came from something that already worked".

**3. Optional bonus: your scoreboard output.** If you ran the scoreboard skill, paste its tiers. Then the 30 descend from your proven winners, which is the strongest possible ground.

## The method

### Step 1. Topic is not angle

This distinction decides whether the output is useful.

- "AI" is a topic.
- "AI replaces an expensive role" is an angle.
- "The expensive part of bookkeeping was always the checking" is an angle.

A topic is what the post is about. An angle is the claim it makes. Two posts on the same topic with different angles perform completely differently, which is why topic-level planning produces flat months.

### Step 2. Number the material

Number every item the user pasted, 1, 2, 3, so every angle produced can point back to its source. If posts were pasted, mark which items already performed.

### Step 3. Name the angle inside each item, in 6 words or fewer

Force the compression. If an item takes fifteen words to state, there are two angles inside it; split them. Attach the post's numbers wherever they exist.

### Step 4. Cluster what repeats

Group angles that make the same argument in different ways. When several strong posts support one argument, call it a recurring theme and give it more weight than one result. A single item is one example; treat it with matching confidence.

### Step 5. Generate 30, each traced to its source

For every new angle, name:

1. The angle, 6 words or fewer
2. The numbered item it descends from
3. What is genuinely new about it, or the honest label "rerun"

If an angle cannot be traced to something in the user's material, leave it out. An untraceable angle is a guess, and the whole point of this skill is to stop guessing. If the material cannot honestly support 30, produce what it supports and say so. Fewer than 5 real items: produce 10 and name the thinness.

### Step 6. Mark the reruns honestly

Some of the 30 should be reruns of your strongest angle. That is not laziness; the strongest accounts return to a proven angle every week or two wearing a new opening. But say which ones are reruns. The user decides how often to repeat; never disguise a repeat as a new idea.

### Step 7. Build the needs-proof-first list

Separately, list any angle the user clearly wants to claim but has nothing real behind yet. These do not go in the 30. For each one, name what would have to be true, done, or measured before the claim becomes honest.

## Output

1. The material, numbered
2. The angle inside each item, with post numbers where known, and any clusters
3. The 30, each with its source item and its "new" or "rerun" label
4. The reruns listed in one place
5. The needs-proof-first list, each with its unlock

## What good looks like

Pick any angle from the 30 and ask: could a competitor who never lived your business post this exact thing? If yes, it is a topic wearing an angle costume; trace it tighter to your material or cut it. The best version of this output reads like the table of contents of a book only you could write.

## Guardrails

- Never produce an angle with no traceable source in the material.
- Never let an angle run past 6 words.
- Never dress a rerun as a new idea.
- An opinion can parent an opinion post, never a results claim. If the angle asserts a result, a real result must sit behind it.
- If the material is thin, say so and deliver 10 strong angles rather than 30 padded ones.
