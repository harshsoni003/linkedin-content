# Example: pattern-audit

Dana Reyes runs Ledgerline, a six-person bookkeeping firm serving owner-led small businesses. She has run it for eleven years. Dana, the firm, the clients, and every number in this example are fictional. Her numbers come from the scoreboard example (see scoreboard-example.md).

## What Dana pasted

> Run pattern-audit on my scoreboard output below.
> Median: 12 comments across 24 posts. Hit = 60+.
> [the full tiered list from scoreboard, worst first]

## What pattern-audit returned

Notice the top three medians sit within a few comments of each other. The median says your openings barely matter. The hit rate says otherwise: every big winner you have came from just two of the six families.

**3. The retire list**

**Bare product title. 5 posts, median 1 comment, zero above Flat.** Your own words, so you recognize it:

> "The Q2 Bookkeeping Bundle is live."

The trap check was run before this call: 4 of the 5 went out mid-burst, which shares some blame. But the one that went out in a calm week ("The E-commerce Sales Tax Checklist is here.") still managed only 4 comments. The family fails even with the excuse removed. Keep the offers; retire the announcement opening. Your next lead post opens first person or corrective instead.

**4. On probation, not retired**

**This X / Your X. 5 posts, 0 hits, but the trap check caught something:** all five are tax and compliance warnings, your heaviest topic. The family and the topic have never been tested apart. Verdict: one more attempt on a money-win topic before this family goes on the retire list.

**5. The one to use more**

**Corrective.** 1 of 4 posts became a Hit, your best rate on a rankable sample, and "Most founders read their P&L wrong." is the proof. You hold beliefs your clients' founders get wrong every week; that is a corrective post each. Small sample, so keep first person (1 of 6) warm behind it, but the next batch leans corrective.
