# Example: visual-brief

Dana Reyes runs Ledgerline, a six-person bookkeeping firm serving owner-led small businesses. She has run it for eleven years. Dana, the firm, the clients, and every number in this example are fictional.

## What Dana pasted

> Write the visual brief for this post.
>
> POST:
> The 23-point check we run on every new client's books.
>
> Last month it caught $48,000 in unfiled sales tax a brand didn't even know it owed. The month before, $9,100 in duplicate vendor payments.
>
> It covers sales tax registrations, revenue recognition, inventory, chargebacks, and everything in between. Takes us about 90 minutes per client.
>
> I turned it into a one-page checklist anyone can run on their own books.
>
> Comment "BOOKS" and I'll send it over.
>
> Comment word: "BOOKS"
>
> Real things I can screenshot: the actual checklist in Google Docs, our internal cleanup tracker (client names can't be shown).

## What visual-brief returned

**1. What the image proves**

The 23-point checklist actually exists, by showing the real document.

**2. The main proof**

The checklist itself. Its number is 23, and that is the only digit that appears on the image. The $48,000 stays in the post body; putting it on the image would create two competing numbers.

**3. Layout, by structure**

Hero screenshot with a callout. A real screenshot of the Google Doc, zoomed so the title and the first six or seven checklist items are readable, with the list visibly continuing past the bottom edge so the reader can see there is more. One item circled: the sales tax registration line. Callout text next to the circle.

Runner-up rejected: single stat block built on $48,000. The number is strong, but the post's promise is the checklist, and a reader who can see the real document trusts the offer more than a reader who sees a big number on a colored card.

**4. Every word of on-image text**

- Headline, top: The 23-point new-client books check
- Callout, beside the circled item: This line finds tax you don't know you owe yet
- Ask line, bottom third: Comment "BOOKS"
- Under the ask line, smaller: and I'll send you the checklist

No other words appear on the image. The checklist items themselves are the screenshot, not typeset copy.

**5. The comment word and its placement**

"BOOKS", in quotes, bottom third, second-largest text on the asset after the headline. One ask. No follow, no repost, no like.

**6. Dimensions and crop-safe notes**

- 4:5 portrait, 1080 x 1350, because the checklist needs vertical room.
- Center crop must keep the headline, the circled item, and the comment line. Keep all three inside the middle 80 percent of the frame.
- Headline and comment line must stay legible at one-third size. If "BOOKS" cannot be read at that size, make it larger.
- The screenshot can lose its bottom edge to the crop; it is supposed to look like it continues.
